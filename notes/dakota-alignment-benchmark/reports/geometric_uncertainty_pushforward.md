# Geometric caster model and lossless uncertainty pushforward

**Status:** mathematical procedure and executable structural contract.  It does
not claim a new measured Dakota caster value.  It is designed to remain useful
when direct road-wheel yaw is unavailable.

This note joins four things that were previously scattered across the Dakota
audit and the related rotation repositories:

1. a three-dimensional wheel/steering-axis model;
2. an explicit error-source census made **before** fitting;
3. source-by-source Jacobian or finite-set pushforward; and
4. a separate intake interface for gross reading and transcription failures.

The governing rule is simple:

> Register every uncertainty source first.  After propagation, every registered
> source must be marked `propagated`, `cancelled_first_order`,
> `retained_symbolically`, `branched`, or `rejected_as_incompatible`.  A source
> is never allowed to disappear merely because its numerical combination rule
> has not yet been chosen.

The current census is
[`data/caster_error_source_census.tsv`](../data/caster_error_source_census.tsv).
The Idriç structural check is
[`examples/autogenerated/dakota-geometric-uncertainty/`](../../../examples/autogenerated/dakota-geometric-uncertainty/).

## The procedure, in the required order

### 1. Fix one observation branch and its conventions

Identify the side, generation, adjustment state, sweep, approach direction,
endpoint order, angle units, and sign conventions.  Raw values remain attached
to evidence IDs.  A numerical value is not yet a random variable merely because
it has been accepted as the intended transcription.

### 1a. Route gross-read and provenance failures through a separate interface

For each raw reading, the intake layer returns exactly one of:

```text
Accept(value, provenance)
RetainAlternatives([(value_1, provenance_1), ...])
RejectIncompatible(reason, provenance)
```

This is where `+0.5` versus `+5`, illegible handwriting, an uncertain sign, an
unmapped correction, or a reading from the wrong adjustment state belongs.  A
gross transcription alternative is not approximated by a wider Gaussian tail.
The interface is now specified and structurally checked; a finished automatic
outlier or OCR adjudicator is deliberately outside this change.

### 1b. Census every error source

Before selecting a fit or computing a combined bar, list every source that can
act on the chosen branch.  Give each source:

- a stable ID and provenance;
- the stage where it enters;
- its dependence scope;
- its current representation;
- the coordinate, map, or set on which it acts; and
- its present propagation disposition.

The census includes reading resolution, common gauge zero, zero drift,
transcription alternatives, state incompatibility, level/reference attitude,
steering-command placement, the nominal 17.4:1 input map, nonlinear rack and
Ackermann effects, path-dependent settling, the wheel normal, the steering
axis, model discrepancy, cross-side dependence, and arithmetic rounding.

This step belongs between defining the observation and choosing the forward
model.  It prevents a convenient model from deciding retroactively which errors
exist.

### 2. Declare the finite geometric forward map

Use a local vehicle frame with `x` forward and `z` upward.  For each side, let:

- `alpha` be the commanded steering-wheel rotation;
- `G(alpha, side, path, state)` be the actual road-wheel yaw supplied by a
  steering-input model;
- `a` be the unit steering-axis direction, a point of `S^2`;
- `n_0` be the initial outward wheel-plane unit normal, also a point of `S^2`;
- `R(a, delta)` be the rotation in `SO(3)` through road-wheel yaw `delta` about
  `a`; and
- `H(n, g, b, ...)` be the gauge projection from wheel normal `n`, gravity or
  reference frame `g`, calibration `b`, and any declared nuisance state to the
  displayed camber.

The observation map is the composition

$$
\alpha
\xrightarrow{\;G\;}
\delta
\xrightarrow{\;R(a,\delta)\;}
n(\delta)
\xrightarrow{\;H\;}
\gamma.
$$

Direct road-wheel yaw is therefore an optional observation of the latent
quantity `delta`, not a prerequisite for defining the estimator.  When yaw is
unavailable, the procedure uses a declared family of possible `G` maps and
reports the resulting model-conditional caster set or unresolved symbolic
source.  It does not pretend that `G` was measured.

### 3. Differentiate the declared map, not a pile of unlabeled numbers

At the selected state, form the named Jacobian of the complete composition.
Every column has a physical or evidentiary meaning and a unit.  For source `i`
with loading or local coordinate map `E_i`, its first-order image is

$$
J_i = DF\,E_i.
$$

Keep these source images separate until their error algebras and dependencies
justify a combination.

### 4. Push each source forward and issue a receipt

Use covariance propagation only for a justified stochastic source, set
propagation for bounds, a formal coefficient map for symbolic epsilon, and
separate model branches for discrete alternatives.  Record first-order
cancellation rather than deleting the source.  Compare the local image with a
finite nonlinear image whenever the source is not demonstrably infinitesimal.

### 5. Combine only compatible objects

Covariances combine through a declared joint covariance.  Simultaneous bounded
sets combine by the appropriate Cartesian/Minkowski or constrained-set
construction.  Symbolic terms remain tagged.  Model branches remain branches.
An incompatible state remains rejected.  There is no universal `+/-` operator.

### 6. Report at the uncertainty scale

The final report states the model branch, nominal result or feasible set,
source-by-source dispositions, unresolved terms, and conditioning diagnostics.
It prints only digits supported by the uncertainty and identifies regression-
oracle decimals as such.

## The trigonometry is a rotation, not an arbitrary sine fit

Rodrigues' formula gives the finite wheel-normal motion:

$$
n(\delta)
=n_0\cos\delta
+(a\times n_0)\sin\delta
+a(a\cdot n_0)(1-\cos\delta).
$$

This immediately separates the response under steering reversal:

$$
\frac{n(\delta)-n(-\delta)}{2}
=(a\times n_0)\sin\delta,
$$

$$
\frac{n(\delta)+n(-\delta)}{2}-n_0
=\{a(a\cdot n_0)-n_0\}(1-\cos\delta).
$$

The first expression is odd in steering angle; the second is even.  This is the
geometric reason the recovered scalar model uses

$$
\gamma(\delta)
\approx \gamma_0+B\sin\delta+K(1-\cos\delta).
$$

`B` is the gauge-visible projection of the odd rotation term.  `K` collects the
gauge-visible even term.  Neither coefficient becomes physical caster merely
because a regression returns it.

For intuition, choose a side-local frame in which the unsteered outward wheel
normal is approximately the lateral basis vector.  The vertical component of
`a x n_0` then contains the fore/aft tilt of the steering axis.  Under the
small-tilt and approximately linear gauge projection used by the service
formula, the odd camber difference is approximately

$$
\Delta\gamma \approx 2\,\chi\,\sin\delta,
$$

where `chi` is caster in a consistent angular unit and the sign depends on the
declared side/frame convention.  This explains

$$
\widehat\chi_{\text{conditional}}
\approx \frac{\gamma_+-\gamma_-}{2\sin\delta}.
$$

The approximation hides steering-axis inclination, the initial wheel normal,
the nonlinear gauge projection, and the command-to-yaw map.  The finite
Rodrigues model shows exactly where those terms live instead of calling all of
them measurement noise.

## The sphere and rotation tangent maps

The wheel normal belongs to `S^2`, not to unconstrained `R^3`, and an
orientation belongs to `SO(3)`, not to nine independent matrix entries.

Represent a small orientation error by `omega` in the Lie algebra `so(3)`, with
skew matrix `[omega]_x`.  Its action on the normal is

$$
dn=\omega\times n=-[n]_\times\omega.
$$

Two structural facts follow:

$$
n\cdot dn=0,
$$

so `dn` lies in the tangent plane `T_nS^2`, and

$$
n\times n=0,
$$

so infinitesimal rotation about the wheel normal is in the first-order kernel
of the normal observation.  The Idriç example checks both identities exactly on
integer samples.

If `omega` has a justified local covariance `Sigma_omega`, then

$$
\Sigma_n
=(-[n]_\times)\Sigma_\omega(-[n]_\times)^T.
$$

This covariance has rank at most two.  That rank defect is geometric
information, not a numerical failure to be patched by adding a small diagonal.

For fixed `a`, differentiation of Rodrigues' formula gives

$$
\frac{dn}{d\delta}=a\times n(\delta).
$$

For a tangent perturbation `da` of the unit axis (`a dot da = 0`), the
directional derivative at fixed `delta` is

$$
D_a n[da]
=(da\times n_0)\sin\delta
+\{da(a\cdot n_0)+a(da\cdot n_0)\}(1-\cos\delta).
$$

If the gauge convention is locally

$$
H(n)=-\arcsin(n\cdot z),
$$

then

$$
d\gamma
=-\frac{z\cdot dn}{\sqrt{1-(n\cdot z)^2}}.
$$

The full derivative is the product of these named tangent maps.  This is the
matrix chain the uncertainty sources must traverse.

## The checked pair Jacobian is one final link in that chain

For endpoint coordinates

$$
q=(\theta_+,\theta_-,\gamma_+,\gamma_-),
$$

define

$$
D=\sin\theta_+-\sin\theta_-,\qquad
N=\gamma_+-\gamma_-,\qquad
C=N/D.
$$

Away from `D=0`, the signed coefficient has Jacobian

$$
\frac{\partial C}{\partial\theta_+}
=-\frac{N\cos\theta_+}{D^2},\qquad
\frac{\partial C}{\partial\theta_-}
= \frac{N\cos\theta_-}{D^2},
$$

$$
\frac{\partial C}{\partial\gamma_+}=\frac1D,
\qquad
\frac{\partial C}{\partial\gamma_-}=-\frac1D.
$$

When angle coordinates are degrees, the two theta derivatives acquire the
factor `pi/180`.  For `|C|`, multiply the row by `sign(C)` away from `C=0`.

Let `x` contain command, ratio, steering-map parameters, steering axis, wheel
normal, gauge state, reference attitude, and latent path state.  Then the
physically meaningful chain is

$$
J_{C,x}=J_{C,q}\,J_{q,x}.
$$

The existing four-entry Jacobian is therefore correct but incomplete: it
propagates uncertainty already expressed in endpoint yaw and camber.  It cannot
by itself account for uncertainty in how the steering command created those
yaws or how a rotating wheel normal created the gauge readings.

## No direct yaw measurement: what is and is not identified

The manual-derived nominal map remains useful:

$$
G_0(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{17.4}.
$$

It yields nominal equal-angle road-wheel magnitudes of about `10.34 degrees`
for a half steering-wheel turn, `20.69 degrees` for one turn, and `31.03
degrees` for one and a half turns.  These are model inputs, not direct yaw
observations.

Without direct yaw, use

$$
G=G_0+\epsilon_G
$$

or a bounded/function-valued family `G ∈ 𝒢`.  Then compute either a
symbolic first-order result containing `epsilon_G` or the feasible set

$$
\{\chi:\text{some }G\in\mathcal G
\text{ and nuisance state reproduce the accepted readings}\}.
$$

This is a usable estimation procedure; it is not a point-identification claim.
Additional steering scales help check `G`.  In the G5 reset sweep, the observed
odd camber signal grew by about `2.8` to `3.0` from half turn to one turn on
both sides, while the nominal proportional sine map predicts a factor near
two.  Because the same failure appears bilaterally, it is evidence against
explaining the disagreement as one isolated bad gauge read.  It does not say
whether the cause is rack geometry, compliance, path/settling state, or another
shared input-map defect.

Direct yaw would narrow `𝒢` if it ever became available, but it is not a
required step in this procedure.

## Nested uncertainty without pretending everything is a ball

The notation

$$
(p),\qquad ((p)),\qquad (((p)))
$$

is represented as a filtration

$$
\mathcal N_1(p)\subseteq
\mathcal N_2(p)\subseteq
\mathcal N_3(p).
$$

No symmetry, probability distribution, common unit, or numerical radius is
implied.  The sets may be skewed, disconnected, or branch-valued.  For any
deterministic map `F`, inclusion alone guarantees

$$
F(\mathcal N_1)
\subseteq F(\mathcal N_2)
\subseteq F(\mathcal N_3).
$$

The images need not resemble the original sets.

When a centered Euclidean input ball of radius `r` is linearized by `J`, its
image is an ellipsoid in `image(J)`.  Its principal output directions are the
left singular vectors of `J` and its semiaxis lengths are `r sigma_i`, where
`sigma_i` are the singular values.  Nullspace directions collapse to zero at
first order and must be recorded as `cancelled_first_order` or
`unobservable_first_order`, not erased.

An exact rotation `R` is orthogonal, so

$$
R(B_r)=B_r.
$$

It rotates a spherical Euclidean error set without squashing it.  The complete
parameter-to-rotation-to-gauge-to-caster Jacobian is not orthogonal; projection,
trigonometric sensitivity, and rank loss can turn the same local ball into a
thin ellipsoid or a lower-dimensional set.  This distinguishes the geometric
certainty of rotation from the conditioning of the measurement.

For an input covariance `Sigma`, write `Sigma=L L^T`.  The corresponding local
ellipsoid is mapped by `J L`, and

$$
\Sigma_{out}=J\Sigma J^T.
$$

For an interval box with radius vector `r`, the first-order image is a zonotope;
the coordinatewise enclosure `|J|r` is conservative but forgets its internal
geometry.  A constrained sphere cap, correlated box, or function family should
be pushed forward as that set instead of first being replaced by independent
coordinate bars.

## Why asymmetric bars require the finite map

A centered symmetric input set remains centrally symmetric under a linear
Jacobian.  A linear map cannot create one-sided uncertainty from a centered
sphere.  Asymmetry comes from at least one of:

- an asymmetric input set;
- a discrete branch;
- a nonlinear map; or
- a reporting transformation such as absolute value near a cusp.

For a smooth finite perturbation `h`,

$$
F(x+h)=F(x)+Jh+\tfrac12D^2F[h,h]+\cdots.
$$

The quadratic term is even in `h`, so it shifts both endpoints in the same
output direction and naturally produces unequal lower and upper deviations.
This is why the historical half-turn angle stress test has a strongly
asymmetric caster interval even though its angle box was symmetric.

The correct practice is dual:

1. keep the Jacobian image as the local explanatory shadow; and
2. push the finite interval/set through the nonlinear trigonometric map for the
   actual enclosure or diagnostic envelope.

## Symbolic epsilon is useful, but it is only the first-order shadow

With a square-zero perturbation,

$$
\sin(\theta+\epsilon)
=\sin\theta+\cos\theta\,\epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon^2=0.
$$

This makes the Jacobian coefficient exact inside the selected first-order
algebra.  It intentionally discards curvature and cannot by itself express an
asymmetric finite image.

Each unresolved source keeps its own label:

$$
x
+c_{read}\epsilon_{read}
+c_G\epsilon_G
+c_{level}\epsilon_{level}.
$$

The formal sum here is tagged bookkeeping, closer to a direct sum `⊕` than
ordinary numerical addition.  No rule for comparing, squaring, or combining
unlike labels is inferred.  The repository still supports both possible
epsilon algebras only when explicitly selected:

- individual squares vanish while mixed products may survive; or
- the whole epsilon ideal squares to zero.

The finite-set layer remains alongside either choice.

## Combination rules by error type

| Error representation | Forward operation | Permitted combination |
|---|---|---|
| Joint stochastic covariance | `J Sigma J^T` | Add source blocks only after zero cross-covariance is asserted; otherwise use one joint covariance. |
| Shared latent systematic | Push its loading through `J` | Keep the latent source once; repetitions do not divide it by sample count. |
| Bounded or feasible set | Exact `F(U)` where practical; `J U` as local shadow | Combine through a declared joint feasible set, not automatic RSS. |
| Symbolic epsilon | Push its named coefficient through `J` | Retain the label unless an explicit algebra and dependence rule says more. |
| Discrete alternative | Evaluate one model/evidence branch per alternative | Preserve a union or branch table; never average merely to get one number. |
| Incompatible state | No forward estimate on the mixed state | Reject the combination and name the failed state key. |
| Model discrepancy | Enlarge/branch the map family or retain a discrepancy term | Do not hide it inside sensor covariance. |
| Arithmetic rounding | Verified numeric bound or arithmetic-error model | Combine according to the operation graph and carrier, with a named receipt. |

## Required checks before a caster/error-bar report

1. **Source conservation:** every census ID has exactly one disposition.
2. **State compatibility:** generation, adjustment state, side, sweep, and
   approach direction match the intended estimator.
3. **Unit and label match:** Jacobian columns and uncertainty coordinates have
   identical names, order, and units.
4. **Sphere invariants:** wheel normals and steering axes remain normalized;
   tangent perturbations are orthogonal to their base vectors.
5. **Rotation invariants:** finite rotations preserve norm and have determinant
   `+1` within the chosen numerical tolerance.
6. **Odd/even reversal:** reversing `delta` flips the odd term and preserves the
   even term.
7. **Scale consistency:** half/full/lock odd signals agree with the declared
   `G` family or cause that branch to be rejected/enlarged.
8. **Common-mode audit:** first-order cancellations are tested and still appear
   in the receipt.
9. **Finite-versus-linear comparison:** any material interval is propagated
   through the finite trigonometric map as well as `J`.
10. **Dependence audit:** reused readings and session/side shared sources are not
    counted as independent evidence.
11. **Precision audit:** carrier and rounding source are recorded; oracle
    decimals are not presented as physical precision.

## Numeric carrier policy

The requested physical path is:

- `Float16` as the default stored measurement carrier where supported;
- explicit promotion to `Float32` for trigonometric evaluation, Jacobians, or
  accumulation when required by the error budget;
- no host `Double`/binary64 as the physical measurement carrier; and
- a named arithmetic-error source in the same census as the mechanical and
  observational sources.

The Haskell endpoint-pair frontend now uses GHC `Float` (IEEE-754 binary32) as
the measurement-facing scalar path, preserves the input literals, and rounds
its conditional center to the surviving reading scale.  It therefore satisfies
the no-binary64 rule for that final scalar link.  R, Ithon, Agda, Idriç, NumPy,
and the other scalar implementations remain binary64 regression/reference
oracles.  The Haskell path does not yet provide default Float16 storage, the
complete command-to-`SO(3)`-to-gauge finite map, or a verified arithmetic bound
for that full graph.  The autogenerated Idriç program avoids silently filling
that gap: its structural checks use exact `Integer` samples, and its separate
`Float32` probe records the pinned compiler boundary instead of aliasing
`Float32` to `Double`.

## Evidence and conversation boundary

This synthesis compared the retrievable Dakota conversation context with the
active repository and the related checked-out repositories above.  The original
complete conversation corpus is not available in the audit package, so it
would be false to claim that every historical utterance was re-read verbatim.
Known missing or ambiguous source material remains `PROV-001` in the census and
is branched rather than guessed.

## What this model can honestly deliver now

It can deliver:

- nominal caster proxies conditional on the 17.4:1 map;
- a source-preserving Jacobian expression;
- finite interval/set images for any supplied bounds;
- explicit symbolic terms for unknown tolerances;
- branch comparisons for transcription and steering-map alternatives;
- odd/even and multi-scale diagnostics; and
- an honest statement of what cancels, what combines, and what remains
  unresolved.

It cannot yet deliver one defensible current physical caster `+/-` one complete
numerical bar.  The missing ingredients are magnitudes or feasible sets for
several census sources, a calibrated or sufficiently bounded steering map, and
the full Float16-stored/Float32 finite-geometric kernel with an arithmetic-error
bound beyond the existing binary32 endpoint-pair path.  Those are visible gaps
in the procedure rather than omissions hidden behind a neat final decimal.
